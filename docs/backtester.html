The backtester, line by line

The project's backtest.py is 157 lines and five functions. This page reads it the way the study guide says to: map it, trace it on six days of made-up prices, label every line, learn the twelve pandas methods it uses, then explain it back. Every number on the page is computed in the page's own JavaScript from the six closes, so any of them can be checked by hand.

Rules this file enforces, and this page respects 1 · Signals use only past data. 2 · The one-bar lag, pos = target.shift(1), is never removed. 3 · Every result includes costs.

The six-day example is invented. No market data, no performance claims.

5 · The formulas, read aloud

  
volt = ewm_stdspan(r)t · √365

  
Line 23. The exponentially weighted standard deviation of daily returns, using only past bars, times the square root of bars per year to quote it annually. Span 30 means λ ≈ 0.935; the EWMA note is about this one line.

  
scalet = voltargetvolt   targett = clip( signalt · scalet, ±max_leverage )

  
Lines 56–57. The risk you want divided by the risk the market is giving: at 10% target and 20% vol, half a unit; at 60% vol, a sixth. Then capped at ±2 units of notional. With vol_target=None the signal is just clipped (line 59).

  
post = targett−1

  
Line 62, the danger line. The position held during day t is the target computed at day t−1's close. Decide tonight, trade tomorrow.

  
turnovert = | post − post−1 |   costt = turnovert · bps104   carryt = | post | · carryannual365

  
Lines 68–70. Turnover is how much the position changed today; a flip from +1 to −1 is 2. Cost is turnover times the per-unit charge (5 bps = 0.0005). Carry is a daily slice of the annual charge for holding any position, long or short.

  
nett = post · rt − costt − carryt   equityt = ∏s≤t (1 + nets)

  
Lines 72–73 and 79. Position times today's return is the gross profit; subtract what trading and holding cost; equity is the running product, the growth of $1.

  
CAGR = equityT365/T − 1   Vol = std(net) · √365   Sharpe = mean(net) · 365Vol

  
Lines 87–94. CAGR turns the final equity into a per-year growth rate (meaningless on six days; the page shows it anyway because the code does). Vol annualises the daily standard deviation. Sharpe is the annualised mean over the annualised standard deviation.

  
drawdownt = equitytmaxs≤t equitys − 1   MaxDrawdown = mint drawdownt

  
Line 86 and 95. How far below its running peak the equity is on each day, and the worst such value. The number a risk page asks for first.







  
6 · stats() and yearly() on the six days

  
The eleven numbers stats returns, computed live from the table above (whichever scenario is selected). Click one to see its line and its arithmetic.





  

    

    

    
yearReturnMaxDD

    
yearly() groups by calendar year. The six example days all fall in one year, so there is one row; on nine years of real data it is the quickest regime check: a rule that worked in two years out of nine is suspect.

  







  
7 · walk_forward() as a picture

  
Lines 116–157. Choose parameters on a training window, trade only the next test window with that choice, slide, repeat, and stitch the test windows together. Click a stage.





  

    

    
    

    
Why its Sharpe is usually lower, and which to trust.A full-history Sharpe lets the parameters see the whole sample, including the years they are then scored on; the best parameter on a history is partly the noise of that history, so the number is inflated. The walk-forward Sharpe only ever uses a choice that was made before the period it is scored on, so it is closer to what paper trading will show. Trust the walk-forward number, and watch the log it returns: if train_sharpe is 2.0 and test_sharpe is 0.3 in every window, the rule is fitting noise. The grid the project plans to walk forward is the lookback blend from the momentum note.

9 · Words used here

  
Each entry: the technical definition first, then the same idea in everyday words in the orange strip.

  

    
Series / DataFramepandas' one-column and many-column tables, indexed by date here.a column of numbers with dates down the side, and a whole spreadsheet of them.

    
vectorisedoperating on whole columns at once instead of looping row by row.one line does the sum for every day; no "for each day" loop.

    
notionalthe money value of a position; a position of 1 = 100% of the account.how big the bet is, measured in multiples of your account.

    
bpsbasis points, 1 bp = 0.01%.a hundredth of a percent. 5 bps on $10,000 is $5.

    
turnoverthe absolute change in position; per year, the sum.how much buying and selling the rule does.

    
carrythe cost of holding a position overnight: funding on a perpetual, financing on a CFD.rent for keeping the position open.

    
leverageposition size in units of the account; 2 means a position worth twice the account.borrowing to bet bigger than your money; the cap keeps it to 2×.

    
drawdownequity divided by its running maximum, minus one.how far below your best-ever point you are.

    
CAGRcompound annual growth rate: the constant yearly rate that would produce the final equity.the average yearly growth, if it had been smooth.

    
out-of-sampledata not used when the parameters were chosen.the part of history the rule never got to peek at.

    
warm-upthe bars a lookback signal needs before it has a value.a 252-day rule says nothing for its first 252 days; walk_forward feeds the training history in so the test starts warm.
