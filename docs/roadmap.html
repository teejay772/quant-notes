Quant notes · 05 research plan

Development, backtesting and out-of-sample evaluation of two long/short models on gold and bitcoin

A daily trend model and an intraday model on XAU/USD and BTC/USD are specified from the literature, backtested net of transaction costs, validated by walk-forward analysis, frozen, and run on a simulated account. This page sets out the sixteen-week schedule, the sources consulted in each week and the deliverable that records it, so that the sequence of work can be checked against the research log.

1 · Structure

Four phases. Phase I builds the data pipeline and the evaluation tools. Phase II develops a daily trend-following model, tests it out of sample and freezes its specification. Phase III runs that model on a simulated account while an intraday model is developed. Phase IV runs both models and produces the written results. The mathematics lane (Shreve, Stochastic Calculus for Finance) runs in parallel and does not block the model work; it is first required in week 11, where an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process is fitted.

3 · Evaluation criteria

Conditions for a result to be reported as credible. An out-of-sample Sharpe ratio, net of costs, within a plausible range: approximately 0.7–1.2 for a daily trend model. Backtest values of 3 or above are treated as evidence of overfitting rather than of skill. Simulated-account results consistent with the backtest: comparable position sizes and turnover, and realised slippage within the cost assumption. A complete research log: each rule recorded before it is tested, negative results retained, and the number of variants tried stated so that the Sharpe ratio can be adjusted for multiple testing. Funding rates, gold trading hours and weekend gaps modelled within the backtest rather than noted outside it.

Results are presented only after three to four months of simulated trading, which places that point around weeks 20–24, beyond the end of this plan. The plan is the preparation; the live log is the evidence.

4 · Terminology

Each entry gives the technical definition, then the same idea in everyday terms in the orange strip.

long / shorta long position gains when the price rises; a short position gains when it falls.a position that benefits from a rise, or one that benefits from a fall. A long/short model can hold either, so it does not depend on the market going up.
simulated accounta broker account that executes orders against live prices with simulated capital; also called a paper or demo account.a practice account with real prices and real order handling but no real money. It tests the execution and the process, not the return.
walk-forwardparameters are chosen on a training window and evaluated on the following, unseen window; the procedure is repeated along the history and the evaluation windows are concatenated.choose the setting on three years of data, apply it to the next year only, move forward a year, repeat. No setting is judged on the data that chose it.
out-of-sampledata not used when the parameters were chosen.the part of the history the rule was not fitted to.
robustnessthe stability of a result under perturbation: neighbouring parameters, higher assumed costs, each instrument alone, different market regimes.whether the result survives small changes. A rule that works only at one exact lookback and one exact cost level is a coincidence.
frozen specificationthe rule, parameters and position sizing recorded in full and held fixed from the start of simulated trading.the method written down before it is run. A later change defines a new model with a new log, not a revision of the old one.
regimea period of the history with distinct statistical character: a crash, a sustained rise, a flat year.a period with its own conditions. A rule evaluated only on favourable periods has not been tested.
live logone record per trading day: signal, target position, fill, slippage, and profit and loss against the backtest's expectation.the daily record that lets a reader check that the model did in practice what the backtest said it would.
Model 1 / Model 2a daily trend-following model and a short-term intraday model on the same two instruments.a slow model that follows moves lasting months, and a fast one that trades within days. They are expected to earn at different times.
